The lab wanted to know how much a building's physical condition, inside and out, actually shows up in rent. Survey and census data covered the indoor side. The outdoor side had no dataset, so I built one.

For each complex I pulled Google Street View at four angles and ran Otsu thresholding in OpenCV to estimate the fraction of each view that was vegetation, averaged into a Green View Index. Joining that with survey violation records and census covariates produced a 53,111-unit dataset across 541 multifamily complexes, from just over a million raw records.

The modeling side was hedonic regression and multilevel random-coefficient models in R, with a Random Forest for feature importance. Greenery ranked seventh of seventeen rent determinants, which was higher than any of us expected for a variable computed from street photos.
